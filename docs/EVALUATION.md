# Evaluation notes

> Everything below is measured on a **simulated** cohort (8,000 patients, 40% with MG) built from published clinical frequencies. It shows how consistent the models are with that simulation. It does not show how they would perform on real patients. Generated by `backend/mg_predictor/evaluate_honest.py`.

Primary model: **Logistic Regression**. Test set: 1,600 patients (640 with MG). Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples).

## 3. What a POSITIVE means at realistic prevalence

Sensitivity 98.6% and specificity 99.3% are taken from the test set above. Predictive values depend on how common MG is in the group being screened. Myasthenia gravis is rare (published prevalence estimates are roughly 10 to 20 per 100,000 people, and vary by country), so the 40% used in the simulation is far from a real screening population.

| MG prevalence in the screened group | PPV (a POSITIVE is truly MG) | NPV | False alarms per true positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| general population (~0.02%) | 2.6% | 100.0% | 37.0 |
| 1% | 57.7% | 100.0% | 0.7 |
| 5% | 87.7% | 99.9% | 0.1 |
| 20% | 97.1% | 99.6% | 0.0 |
| 40% (the simulated cohort) | 98.9% | 99.1% | 0.0 |

In practice this means the tool is only sensible for people who already have suspicious symptoms. Applied to the general population, almost every POSITIVE would be a false alarm.

## 5. Robustness to noisy answers

Every yes/no answer in the test set was flipped at random with the probability shown (training data contains only 1.2% answer noise).

| Answers flipped | Accuracy | Sensitivity | Specificity | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0% | 99.0% | 98.6% | 99.3% | 0.997 |
| 5.0% | 94.4% | 98.6% | 91.7% | 0.993 |
| 10.0% | 87.9% | 97.5% | 81.5% | 0.978 |
| 20.0% | 73.1% | 95.9% | 57.8% | 0.919 |

## Takeaway

On the simulated cohort the models look strong, but that mostly reflects how cleanly the simulation separates the classes. The numbers that matter for any real use are the ones this simulation cannot provide: performance on real, de-identified patients from the population the tool is meant for.
